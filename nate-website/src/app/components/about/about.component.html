How I came to Mathematics

I firmly decided to pursue mathematics after being a software developer for a year. Software development was a natural path for me as I have been programming for years and loved the feeling of the world being "open" to you if you knew how to code and the infrastructure of computers. I had the incredible opportunity to join a very talented team and was trusted to work on some amazing projects that touched on so many fields of computer science. But it was this experience away from mathematics that made me realize just how much I missed it, and so I decided I must give a fair shot at pursuing it. This still felt like a compromise to my multitude of other interests; however, I am fortunate that mathematics fractals into a myriad of fields, each promising a lifetime of interesting content.

What I work on

Choosing a specific field of mathematics research was very challenging: To help decide I did research projects in different fields of mathematics during my undergraduate summers; one in representation theory, one in non-linear wave equations around singularities, and one in Geometric Topology. My experience was always positive, and to coalesce my interests as much as possible, I decided to pursue algebraic geometry. I am currently drawn towards Hodge Theory, Anabelian Geometry, and aspects of Langlands, but I have yet to dive into enough topics to be sure I will end up researching within these fields.

Alongside research, I love reading through textbooks and papers, and when inspiration strikes I write essays on the intuitions I gain (see the blog). I try to compile what I learn into a series of textbooks called Everything You Need To Know About (EYNTKA) (click here), and for standalone projects I keep notes. I also tutor mathematics.
