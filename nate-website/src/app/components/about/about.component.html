Previous Research

To help decide research interests, I did projects in different fields of mathematics during my undergraduate summers: one in representation theory, one in non-linear wave equations around singularities, and one in Geometric Topology. My experience was always positive, and to coalesce my interests as much as possible, I decided to pursue Algebraic Geometry. I am currently drawn towards Hodge Theory, Anabelian Geometry, and aspects of Langlands, but I have yet to dive into enough topics to be sure I will end up researching within these fields.

My first PhD project asked whether twisted K-theory strengthens the topological obstruction to the integral Hodge conjecture. I wanted to settle what structure a cohomology theory needs in order to factor the cycle map, which led to showing that twisting gives no additional information, and moves the question toward algebraic obstructions.

Alongside research, I try to compile what I learn into a series of textbooks called Everything You Need To Know About (EYNTKA) (click here), and for standalone projects I keep notes.

Comp-Sci

I decided to pursue mathematics after being a software developer for around two years. Software development was a natural path for me as I loved how coding made the world "open" to you if you knew the infrastructure of computers. Fulfilling this path, I had the incredible opportunity to join a talented team and worked on some amazing projects that touched many fields of computer science. But, I realized that the aspect of the job I liked the most was creating the frameworks other systems depend on, similar to how mathematics creates the mental framework for computer science and physics. Combined with my interest in teaching and research, I decided I must give a fair shot at pursuing it.
