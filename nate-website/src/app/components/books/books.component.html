The Everything You Need To Know About (EYNTKA) series

I found that writing down what I learn as though I'm writing a textbook has been incredibly helpful in internalizing what I learn, and has produced these books as a by-product. They have become my foundations for finding references and interweaving interesting connections I found. I named my series of books "Everything You Need To Know About" (EYNTKA) as they contain what I believed was the "core" material for the subject matter, being limited in material by my time to write down results. I update my books on a regular basis with new information and fixes.

Notes & caveats
  • None of these books would be considered "first edition"; there is a lack of exercises and often a lack of figures that I should draw.
  • Unsurprisingly, I learn faster than I can write, so there are some gaps in the books where I decided I want to include material but have not had the time to fill it in. This is in line with the above comment that none of the books should be considered "first edition".
  • I don't have books on the foundations of modern mathematics or calculus. There are many great books that cover the foundational material; three that come to mind are Brendan W. Sullivan's Everything You Always Wanted To Know About Mathematics Shay Fuchs's Introduction to Proofs and Proof Strategies, and Martin Liebecks a concise introduction to prue mathematics. For calculus, there are so many options that it feels like an injustice to single one out, but if I had to explicitly recommend one, the one that I used most often when teaching calculus is Stewart's calculus. For algebra, I have some notes outlining the necessary material that can be found here.
  • As I have control over all of these books, I have the liberty to cross reference material between them. Due to this, the organization of the material is chosen to as best as possible fit a "narrative" that works well between the books. Checkout the series map to see which books depend on which.
  • Some of these books have overlapping content as the material is worth having in both places for completion (ex. elliptic curves are covered in both EYNTKA Complex Analysis and Elliptic Curves, fundamental groups are in EYTNKA Topology and Algebraic Topology, Riemann Roch in Algebraic Geometry and Complex Geometry, Banach and Hilbert spaces in ETYNKA functional analysis and Real Analysis, etc.).
  • On the other hand, since the EYNTKA series serves multiple disciplines of mathametics, I sometimes decide to have material at the lowest comon denominator of many books. For example, topological groups are covered in the foundational topology book as the material is important for analysis, representation theory, and geometry all at once.
  • Disclaimer: apologies for any typos in the books! Feel free to send me any errata (see contact).
